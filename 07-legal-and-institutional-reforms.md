# Legal and institutional reform agenda

## Purpose

This chapter identifies the legal mechanisms needed to deliver the economic program. It is policy analysis, not legal advice. Before drafting amendments, Tunisian counsel should verify the consolidated Arabic and French texts, later amendments, decrees, circulars and constitutional procedure.

The recommended approach is targeted. Tunisia already has budget, investment, procurement, PPP, competition and renewable-energy frameworks. The recurring gap is between principle and implementation: missing secondary rules, overlapping approvals, weak disclosure, insufficient regulatory independence, slow appeals and no consequence for missed deadlines.

## 1. Organic Budget Law

### Current foundation

Organic Law no. 2019-15 of 13 February 2019 establishes principles of transparency and sincerity, mission- and program-based budgeting, a medium-term framework and supporting information on debt, transfers and public bodies.

Source: [Organic Budget Law no. 2019-15](https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/loi-organique-n-2019-15-du-13-fevrier-2019-relative-a-la-loi-organique-du-budget/).

### Required implementation and amendments

1. **Fiscal-risk statement:** require annual disclosure of public-enterprise exposure, guarantees, PPP commitments, arrears, litigation, natural-disaster risk and financial-sector contingent liabilities.
2. **Tax-expenditure report:** identify every exemption and preferential rate, legal basis, beneficiary, estimated cost, objective and sunset.
3. **Project pipeline annex:** show lifecycle cost, readiness, financing, procurement, schedule and operating provision for major projects.
4. **Guarantee expected loss:** move beyond a gross ceiling to probability-weighted fiscal cost and sector concentration.
5. **Performance reporting:** require outputs and outcomes for each program, with data definitions and independent audit.
6. **Supplementary-budget triggers:** specify when energy prices, growth, inflation or financing deviations require a revised budget.
7. **Arrears rule:** define arrears, require monthly reporting, and prevent commitments without available authorization.
8. **Citizen format and open data:** release machine-readable budget and execution data.

### Proposed statutory rule

Any bill creating a permanent expenditure, tax exemption, guarantee or regulated-price obligation should include:

- five-year fiscal cost;
- funding source;
- distributional assessment;
- implementation authority;
- measurable objective;
- expiry or review date; and
- compatibility with the medium-term fiscal framework.

## 2. Central Bank statute and monetary financing

### Current foundation

Law no. 2016-35 establishes the Central Bank statute. The 2026 Finance Law authorizes an exceptional direct facility as a derogation from article 25.

Sources: [Central Bank statute](https://www.juridoc.tn/fr/document/loi-n--2016-35-du-25-avril-2016-portant-fixation-du-statut-de-la-banque-centrale-de-tunisie/3024), [2026 Finance Law](https://www.finances.gov.tn/sites/default/files/2026-01/115725.pdf).

### Reform

The law should preserve a narrow emergency mechanism but prevent normalization:

- cumulative nominal and GDP-relative ceiling;
- defined eligible emergency uses;
- supermajority or enhanced parliamentary procedure for renewal;
- expiry after a short statutory period;
- monthly disclosure of stock, flow, use and repayment;
- BCT opinion on inflation, liquidity and reserves;
- government exit and financing plan;
- no automatic rollover;
- no financing of new permanent recurrent commitments; and
- independent audit.

This keeps democratic authority over exceptional financing while protecting monetary credibility.

## 3. Electricity and renewable energy

### Current foundation

Law no. 2015-12 governs electricity generation from renewable energy. Decree no. 2016-1123 provides implementing rules. The self-production regime allows eligible users to produce electricity and, under conditions, sell surplus.

Sources: [Renewable-electricity law](https://www.ecolex.org/details/legislation/loi-n-2015-12-du-11-mai-2015-relative-a-la-production-delectricite-a-partir-des-energies-renouvelables-lex-faoc181322/), [Energy Ministry self-production regime](https://www.energiemines.gov.tn/fr/themes/energies-renouvelables/projets-et-programmes/regime-de-lautoproduction/).

### Reform package

#### Independent electricity regulator act

Create a regulator with:

- independent board appointment and removal criteria;
- own budget and technical staff;
- authority over tariff methods, service quality, grid codes, connection disputes and market monitoring;
- publication of decisions and models;
- conflict-of-interest and cooling-off rules;
- judicial review; and
- annual parliamentary reporting.

#### Modernized grid and market rules

- transparent connection-capacity map and queue;
- binding study and connection deadlines;
- curtailment and compensation rules;
- technical codes for solar, wind, storage and distributed resources;
- legal status for battery storage, aggregators and demand response;
- standardized PPAs and direct agreements;
- bankable change-in-law and termination provisions;
- competitive procurement and bid disclosure;
- balancing and ancillary-service markets introduced in stages;
- clear self-consumption, wheeling and surplus-sale rules; and
- cyber and physical-security standards.

#### Consumer protection

- lifeline tariff and eligibility rules;
- notice and appeal before disconnection;
- service-quality compensation;
- transparent billing and meter testing;
- installment rules for vulnerable users;
- data privacy for smart meters; and
- automatic review of tariff affordability.

## 4. Public-enterprise governance

### Current foundation

Law no. 89-9 of 1 February 1989 governs state participations and public enterprises. The framework should be consolidated and modernized rather than managed through fragmented decrees and annual rescue decisions.

Source: [Law no. 89-9](https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/loi-n-89-9-du-01-fevrier-1989-relative-aux-participations-et-entreprises-publiques/).

### Proposed public-enterprise governance law

1. Publish a state ownership policy.
2. Create a central ownership and monitoring entity distinct from sector regulation.
3. Define commercial, strategic and public-service objectives.
4. Use skill-based, transparent board nomination.
5. Require audit committees and conflict rules.
6. Publish annual audited accounts to a fixed deadline.
7. Apply recognized accounting standards and disclose quasi-fiscal activities.
8. Separate policy compensation from commercial revenue.
9. Require five-year performance contracts with service and financial targets.
10. Publish debt, arrears, guarantees, procurement and related-party transactions.
11. Require restructuring plans before new state support.
12. Establish rules for merger, concession, minority participation, sale or closure.

### Public-service obligation contract

When government requires an enterprise to supply below cost or serve an uneconomic route, the contract should state:

- service and beneficiary;
- efficient cost benchmark;
- tariff or user contribution;
- budget compensation;
- service-quality indicators;
- verification and audit; and
- expiry or rebid.

This turns hidden cross-subsidy into visible policy.

## 5. Public procurement and open contracting

### Current foundation

Decree no. 2014-1039 regulates public procurement and supports planning, competition and electronic procedures through TUNEPS. It was later amended.

Source: [Public procurement decree](https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/decret-n-2014-1039-du-13-mars-2014-portant-reglementation-des-marches-publics/).

### Reform

- publish planning, tender, award, contract, beneficial owner, implementation and completion data in an open standard;
- disclose all variations, claims and time extensions;
- set emergency-procurement thresholds, justification and ex-post audit;
- use lifecycle cost and quality where lowest initial price creates risk;
- introduce performance-based maintenance contracts where suitable;
- create framework agreements for standard grid, water and medical spares;
- establish professional procurement accreditation;
- use independent review with rapid interim relief;
- measure competition, cycle time, cost growth and supplier payment;
- prohibit contract splitting and undisclosed subcontracting; and
- publish debarment with due process.

The 2025 labor prohibition on abusive subcontracting should not prevent transparent specialist subcontracting needed for complex infrastructure. Procurement documents should require disclosure of the full delivery chain, worker protections and responsibility of the prime contractor.

## 6. Public–private partnerships

### Current foundation

Law no. 2015-49 and Decree no. 2016-772 provide the PPP framework.

Sources: [PPP Law no. 2015-49](https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/loi-n-2015-49-du-27-novembre-2015-relative-aux-contrats-de-partenariat-entre-secteurs-public-et-prive/), [PPP implementing decree](https://legislation-securite.tn/latest-laws/decret-gouvernemental-n-2016-772-du-20-juin-2016-fixant-les-conditions-et-les-procedures-doctroi-des-contrats-de-partenariat-public-prive/).

### Reform

A PPP should not be selected because it delays recorded expenditure. Before tender:

- compare with conventional public procurement;
- publish demand and affordability tests;
- model foreign-exchange, termination and revenue risk;
- identify government guarantees and minimum-revenue commitments;
- obtain independent fiscal-risk review;
- set a lifecycle budget ceiling;
- disclose the signed contract with narrow commercial redactions;
- publish performance and payments; and
- include step-in, handback and dispute rules.

All PPP liabilities should appear in the fiscal-risk statement and project pipeline.

## 7. Investment law and authorizations

### Current foundation

Law no. 2016-71 establishes the investment framework. Tunisia’s market-access portal states the principle that investment is free while identifying activities subject to authorization.

Sources: [Investment Law no. 2016-71](https://www.finances.gov.tn/sites/default/files/reglementaire_fr/Loi2016_71.pdf), [TIA legal framework](https://www.tia.gov.tn/en/legal/2), [market-access portal](https://www.acces-aumarche.gov.tn/ar/legal-framework).

### Reform

1. Replace each authorization with a notification unless a documented public risk requires prior approval.
2. Publish the legal basis, documents, fee, criteria, deadline and appeal.
3. Prohibit agencies from requesting data already held by government.
4. Use a unique project identifier.
5. Apply silence-as-consent to low-risk cases.
6. Require explicit written decisions for health, safety, environment, land and critical infrastructure.
7. Provide a rapid administrative appeal and compensation for unlawful delay where appropriate.
8. Publish processing time and approval outcomes.
9. Give incentives only after a cost-benefit and additionality test.
10. Sunset incentives automatically unless evaluation supports renewal.

## 8. Foreign-exchange code

### Current status

The government approved a proposed new foreign-exchange code in 2024, but parliamentary reporting in June 2026 indicated that the bill was still under consideration. It should therefore be treated as pending, not law in force.

Sources: [Prime Ministry proposal](https://pm.gov.tn/fr/article/finances-adoption-du-code-des-changes-lors-dun-conseil-ministeriel), [June 2026 parliamentary reporting](https://www.lapresse.tn/2026/06/17/code-des-changes-le-parlement-pret-a-passer-au-vote-sans-la-banque-centrale/).

### Recommended content

- move from general prohibition and exceptions toward permitted transactions with defined safeguards;
- guarantee documented current-account payments for legitimate business;
- allow regulated export-retention accounts;
- provide workable cards and payment limits for startups and digital services;
- simplify diaspora investment and repatriation;
- publish bank processing deadlines and reasons for refusal;
- use risk-based AML/CFT controls and data analytics;
- phase capital-account liberalization according to reserves, banking resilience and macro stability;
- empower the BCT to deploy temporary, transparent safeguards during stress; and
- require periodic review by Parliament.

Full, immediate capital-account opening would be risky with limited reserves and fiscal dominance. The objective is predictable productive transactions first, gradual financial liberalization second.

## 9. Competition and prices

### Current foundation

Law no. 2015-36 governs competition and prices.

Source: [Competition and Prices Law no. 2015-36](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/fr/legislation/details/19840).

### Reform

- strengthen the Competition Council’s appointment security, budget and investigative staff;
- provide access to procurement, customs, tax and market data under due process;
- establish merger-review thresholds and deadlines;
- prioritize cartels, bid rigging, abuse in essential markets and restrictive professional rules;
- publish market studies on food distribution, cement, transport, telecom and finance;
- review exclusive rights and entry restrictions;
- require regulatory-impact analysis for new restrictions; and
- separate consumer protection from discretionary price suppression.

## 10. Labor law

### Current change

Law no. 2025-9 amended labor rules, prohibiting labor subcontracting and sharply limiting fixed-term contracts. The social purpose is to prevent disguised employment and permanent precarity. Ambiguous application, however, can make genuine seasonal, specialist and project work uncertain.

Source: [Official Gazette no. 61 of 2025](https://lake.jort.tn/journal-officiel/fr/2025/061.pdf).

### Implementing balance

Secondary rules should:

- define labor-only subcontracting versus a genuine service contract;
- preserve joint liability for wages, safety and social contributions;
- permit specialized services with independent management and deliverables;
- define seasonal and objectively time-limited project work;
- prevent repeated contracts used to avoid permanent status;
- require disclosure of subcontractors and workforce;
- create a rapid labor-inspector or tribunal determination;
- protect whistleblowers and collective rights; and
- apply proportionate transition periods where contracts predate the law.

The economic objective is not deregulation. It is enforceable worker protection with predictable categories.

## 11. Insolvency, secured credit and commercial justice

Investment will remain constrained if viable firms cannot restructure and creditors cannot predict recovery.

Recommended measures:

- early confidential restructuring before payment default;
- temporary stay subject to creditor protection;
- debtor-in-possession or rescue finance with priority rules;
- specialized commercial judges and trained administrators;
- strict procedural timelines;
- electronic case publication;
- movable-collateral registry and clear priority;
- out-of-court workout guidelines for banks;
- second-chance rules for honest entrepreneurs; and
- data on duration, recovery and survival.

## 12. Institutional architecture

### Cabinet Economic Delivery Committee

Chaired at head-of-government level, it should decide cross-ministry issues, review dashboards and remove barriers. It should not award contracts or replace statutory regulators.

### Independent Fiscal Council

A small technical body should assess macro assumptions, fiscal forecasts, rule compliance, tax expenditures, guarantees and long-term sustainability. It should not set the budget.

### Electricity regulator

Independent, technical and transparent, as described above.

### State ownership entity

Professional oversight of public enterprises, distinct from sector policy and regulation.

### Infrastructure and PPP fiscal-risk unit

Located in the Finance Ministry, with authority to challenge affordability and contingent-liability assumptions before procurement.

### Administrative appeals

Fast, published decisions on permits, procurement and economic regulation reduce uncertainty without weakening substantive standards.

## Drafting test for every reform

Before adoption, every legal change should answer:

- What failure does it correct?
- Who must act?
- By when?
- What data must be published?
- What is the appeal?
- How is compliance enforced?
- What does implementation cost?
- What happens to existing contracts?
- What safeguard protects vulnerable groups?
- When will the rule be evaluated or expire?

If these questions are unanswered, another high-level law may add uncertainty rather than resolve it.
