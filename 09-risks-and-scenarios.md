# Risks and scenarios

## Why scenarios are necessary

The reform plan operates under uncertainty about energy prices, rainfall, external demand, tourism, financing, exchange rates, project execution and social acceptance. A single forecast is not enough. The numbers below are policy-planning ranges, not forecasts.

## Scenario 1 — managed recovery

### Assumptions

- Growth remains near 2.5% in 2026 and gradually rises.
- Summer reliability measures reduce major outages.
- TEREG and water disburse on schedule.
- Targeted transfers are operational before price reform.
- External concessional finance improves.
- Domestic borrowing pressure begins to decline.

### Outcomes

- Debt peaks near the low-to-mid 80s as a share of GDP and then declines.
- Fiscal deficit falls below 4% around 2028.
- Renewable and grid investment lowers energy imports and operating cost.
- Private investment conversion improves.
- Employment declines slowly at first and faster after investment matures.

### Policy

Proceed with the full staged program and use upside revenue for arrears, debt and infrastructure.

## Scenario 2 — energy and climate shock

### Assumptions

- Higher gas/oil prices or supply disruption.
- Another extreme heat event raises peak demand.
- Drought increases pumping and desalination needs.
- Regional electricity imports are constrained.

### Outcomes

- Wider trade and fiscal deficits.
- Higher inflation and subsidy cost.
- Lower reserve coverage.
- More pressure on STEG and water utilities.

### Response

- activate demand response and protected-load protocol;
- accelerate efficiency and critical storage;
- target transfers rather than suppress all prices;
- revise the budget openly;
- defer import-intensive low-readiness projects;
- secure concessional emergency finance tied to energy and water resilience;
- protect maintenance and essential imports.

## Scenario 3 — financing stress

### Assumptions

- External disbursements are delayed.
- Domestic banks reach concentration limits.
- refinancing cost or maturity pressure rises;
- confidence in monetary financing weakens.

### Outcomes

- arrears increase;
- private credit falls;
- exchange and inflation pressure rises;
- capital projects slow.

### Response

- publish a financing and arrears plan immediately;
- intensify liability management and concessional refinancing;
- limit new guarantees and low-return commitments;
- accelerate project cancellations where readiness is poor;
- prohibit new permanent spending without funding;
- use any central-bank facility only within the disclosed ceiling and exit plan;
- seek a broader credible policy-financing framework without weakening social safeguards.

## Scenario 4 — reform without protection

### Assumptions

- Fuel, electricity or food prices rise before cash support works.
- Public-enterprise restructuring is treated mainly as layoffs.
- regional access and female employment constraints are ignored.

### Outcomes

- poverty and inflation increase;
- reforms lose legitimacy;
- implementation pauses or reverses;
- fiscal savings are smaller than planned.

### Response

- pause the next adjustment;
- correct beneficiary data and payment channels;
- restore the lifeline block;
- publish the distributional failure;
- redesign the reform and timeline;
- retain operational and transparency reforms that do not harm households.

## Scenario 5 — project boom without control

### Assumptions

- Multiple strategic projects proceed simultaneously.
- Demand and affordability appraisals are weak.
- Guarantees and PPP liabilities are underreported.
- construction costs rise and operating budgets are omitted.

### Outcomes

- debt and foreign-exchange exposure increase;
- incomplete assets compete for funding;
- maintenance is crowded out;
- public confidence declines.

### Response

- apply the project gateway retroactively before irreversible commitments;
- rank projects by readiness and economic return;
- publish full lifecycle and contingent costs;
- pause projects that fail the affordability test;
- complete the highest-return near-finished projects first.

## Scenario 6 — successful acceleration

### Assumptions

- Electricity reliability improves rapidly.
- FX and permit reforms reduce waiting time.
- renewables, water and logistics projects execute well;
- Europe-facing manufacturing and digital services expand;
- diaspora and private capital respond.

### Outcomes

- growth moves above 3.5% before 2030;
- employment and tax revenue improve;
- debt declines faster;
- energy deficit narrows;
- the state can invest more without destabilizing finance.

### Policy

Do not convert temporary upside into permanent recurrent spending. Accelerate debt reduction, education, maintenance, climate resilience and project preparation.

## Risk register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Early warning | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Severe heat and power shortage | High | High | Reserve, forced outages, import availability | Demand response, repairs, storage, protected loads |
| Energy-price shock | Medium-high | High | Import unit prices and subsidy accrual | Targeted support, efficiency, hedging policy, renewables |
| Drought and water interruption | High | High | Reservoirs, groundwater, service events | Leakage reduction, reuse, desalination, drought triggers |
| Fiscal financing gap | Medium-high | High | auctions, arrears, cash balance, reserves | Cash plan, concessional finance, liability management |
| Inflation resurgence | Medium | High | food/free-price/core measures | competition, supply, targeted transfers, fiscal-monetary coordination |
| Social rejection | Medium-high | High | transfer failures, protests, poverty indicators | compensation first, consultation, pause triggers |
| Major-project delay | High | Medium-high | disbursement and physical progress | gateway, delivery unit, open contracting |
| Public-enterprise call | High | High | arrears, debt, guarantees, service failure | audited accounts, performance contracts, explicit subsidy |
| Banking-sovereign feedback | Medium-high | High | sovereign exposure, NPLs, private credit | reduce domestic absorption, stress test, capital planning |
| FX pressure | Medium | High | reserves, parallel premium, current account | phased liberalization, export support, credible macro framework |
| Governance/procurement failure | Medium | High | single bids, variations, complaints | open contracting, audit, appeals, beneficial ownership |
| Global/EU slowdown | Medium | Medium-high | export orders, tourism, remittances | market and product diversification |

## Robust actions across all scenarios

The following actions remain sensible in almost every scenario:

- publish operational and fiscal data;
- protect critical electricity and water loads;
- reduce technical losses and leakage;
- target transfers accurately;
- stop new arrears;
- improve project readiness and procurement;
- finance maintenance;
- simplify legitimate investment operations;
- strengthen competition and appeals;
- train against verified employer demand; and
- disclose guarantees and public-enterprise risk.

These should begin immediately even if political agreement on more difficult structural reforms takes longer.
